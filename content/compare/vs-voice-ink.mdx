Pick **VoiceInk** if you want an open-source dictation app that runs on your Mac, puts text into the field you're in, and costs $25 once. Pick **Whisperer** if you want the same kind of offline dictation for $2.99, and also want [meeting notes without a bot](/meetings), your own AI and an [MCP server](/mcp) for a one-time $14.99 Pro purchase.

## The short version

VoiceInk and Whisperer start from the same place. Both run Whisper-family models on Apple Silicon, both work with no internet, and neither charges a subscription. For dictation alone, they are close.

The difference is scope. VoiceInk is a dictation app. Whisperer is a dictation app plus a meeting recorder: it notices when a Zoom, Teams, Meet, Slack huddle, Webex or FaceTime call starts, records both sides, names speakers from your calendar, and writes notes with decisions and action items. If you never sit in meetings, that difference may not matter to you.

## Where VoiceInk wins

- **Text goes into the field.** VoiceInk pastes for you. Whisperer's App Store build copies the text, and you press ⌘V. That is one extra key on every dictation.
- **Open source.** You can read the code, build it yourself for free, and check exactly what it does with your audio.
- **Older macOS.** VoiceInk runs on macOS 14.4 and later. Whisperer needs macOS 26 Tahoe.
- **Per-app behaviour.** VoiceInk can change its settings depending on the app you're in. Whisperer uses one setup everywhere.
- **Focus.** If all you want is dictation, VoiceInk has fewer screens and nothing about meetings to ignore.

## Where Whisperer wins

- **Meeting notes in the same app.** Whisperer sees which app opened the microphone, asks "Zoom call started. Take notes?", or records on its own for meetings on your [calendar](/calendar). It records the call audio and your microphone, so both sides are captured with speakers or headphones. Nothing joins the call.
- **Who said what.** Speakers are identified on your Mac and named from the calendar invite in one click.
- **Notes you can use.** After the call you get a title, an overview, key topics, decisions, action items with owners, open questions and the next meeting. Ask AI answers questions about a meeting with timestamped sources.
- **Your AI or none.** Notes come from the built-in Qwen3.5 4B model, offline, or from the Claude, ChatGPT, Codex or Gemini plan you already pay for. Read about [your own AI](/ai).
- **MCP.** Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, VS Code and others can search your meetings and dictation history through a server that only apps on your Mac can reach.
- **A cheaper start.** Dictation costs $2.99, file transcription is included, and the dictionary learns words from the corrections you make.

## Who should choose which

**Choose VoiceInk if** you want open-source software, you're on macOS 14 or 15, or you want text placed straight into the app with no ⌘V.

**Choose Whisperer if** you're on macOS 26, you spend part of your week in calls, and you want dictation and meeting notes in one app you pay for once. Start with [dictation](/features), and add Pro when you need meetings.

## FAQ

### Is Whisperer a good VoiceInk alternative?

For dictation alone the two are close peers: both run speech recognition on your Mac and both are one-time purchases. Pick Whisperer if you also want meeting notes, speaker names from your calendar, your own AI and an MCP server. Pick VoiceInk if you want open-source code or text placed straight into the app you're using.

### How much does VoiceInk cost?

VoiceInk is a one-time purchase: Solo $25 for one Mac, Personal $39 for two, Extended $49 for three (public pricing, November 2026). Whisperer is $2.99 one-time, plus $14.99 one-time for Pro.

### Do both apps work offline?

Yes. Both transcribe on your Mac with local models, so dictation works with no internet connection.

### Does Whisperer type into my apps like VoiceInk?

No. In the Mac App Store version, Whisperer copies your text and shows a "Copied ⌘V" toast, and you paste with ⌘V. VoiceInk puts the text into the field for you.
