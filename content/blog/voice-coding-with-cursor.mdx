Cursor turns most coding into a conversation: you describe a change, the agent writes it, you review. The slow part left is typing the description. Speaking it is quicker for most people, and it tends to produce longer, clearer prompts.

<Callout type="info">
Typical conversational speech runs around 130 to 150 words per minute. Average typing speed is often quoted at about 40 words per minute. Your numbers will differ, but for a paragraph-long prompt the gap is large.
</Callout>

## Setup

<StepList>
<Step num="1" title="Install Whisperer">
From the Mac App Store, $2.99. Needs Apple Silicon and macOS 26 Tahoe.
</Step>
<Step num="2" title="Pick your shortcut">
The default is hold Fn. You can choose another key, and switch to toggle mode for long prompts.
</Step>
<Step num="3" title="Add your project vocabulary">
Put library and service names in the [personal dictionary](/features/personal-dictionary) so they come out right.
</Step>
<Step num="4" title="Speak, then paste">
Click into Cursor's prompt box, hold Fn, speak, release. Whisperer copies the text and shows a Copied ⌘V toast. Press ⌘V and send.
</Step>
</StepList>

No Cursor-specific setup is needed. Whisperer works through the clipboard, so it works in Cursor the same way it works everywhere else.

## What about writing code directly?

Whisperer writes what you say as text. It doesn't turn "open paren" into `(` or "camel case" into camelCase. In Cursor you rarely need that: describe the change and the agent writes the syntax. For code you edit by hand, use the keyboard. See [how to dictate code on Mac](/blog/how-to-dictate-code-on-mac) for why this split works better.

## Tips

<FeatureGrid>
<FeatureCard title="Say more than you'd type" icon="target">
Mention edge cases, constraints and what not to touch. It costs seconds by voice.
</FeatureCard>
<FeatureCard title="Fix names once" icon="book-open">
If "Supabase" keeps coming out as "Supa base", add a dictionary entry. It's fixed in every app.
</FeatureCard>
<FeatureCard title="Commit messages" icon="file-text">
After the agent finishes, speak the commit message into Cursor's source control box.
</FeatureCard>
<FeatureCard title="Toggle mode for long prompts" icon="refresh-cw">
Press once to start, once to stop, so you're not holding a key.
</FeatureCard>
</FeatureGrid>

## Give Cursor your meetings and dictations

With Pro, Whisperer runs an optional local [MCP server](/mcp), and Cursor can connect to it with one click. Cursor can then search your meeting transcripts and dictation history. Ask it to "read Tuesday's planning call and draft the ticket for the export feature" and it works from what was actually said. Setup is in [Cursor MCP for meetings](/blog/cursor-mcp-meetings).

Cursor already cuts the amount of code you type. Speaking the prompts cuts the typing that's left. If your wrists are sore, that combination can make a real difference. See [RSI prevention for developers](/blog/rsi-prevention-voice-coding).
</Callout>
