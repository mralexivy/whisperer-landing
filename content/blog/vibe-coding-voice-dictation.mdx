Vibe coding means describing what you want and letting an AI write the code. Cursor, Claude Code, Codex and Windsurf can build whole features from a few paragraphs of instructions. The one part still done by hand is writing those paragraphs.

Speaking them is faster for most people, and it gives your hands a break. Here's how to set it up on a Mac with Whisperer.

## What vibe coding looks like

You give high-level instructions, the agent plans and writes the code, you review and redirect. The tools:

## Why voice fits

### The bottleneck is explaining

The AI is fast. What slows you down is describing the change clearly. Conversational speech is commonly measured at around 130 to 150 words per minute; typing for most people is well under half that. When your input is paragraphs of instructions, that gap shows up on every prompt.

### You say more when you speak

Typed: "add user auth". Spoken: "Add email and password sign-in, hash passwords with bcrypt, and rate-limit login attempts per IP." The spoken version is the one the agent gets right first time.

### Your hands rest

Vibe coding is constant prompt writing. Moving that to voice takes a large share of a day's typing off your wrists.

## Setup

<StepList>
<Step num="1" title="Get Whisperer">
[Mac App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/whisperer-voice-to-text/id6758626671), $2.99. Apple Silicon and macOS 26 Tahoe.

Hold Fn by default. Use toggle mode (press once to start, once to stop) for long prompts.

Framework, library and service names go in the [personal dictionary](/features/personal-dictionary) so they're spelled right.

Hold Fn, talk, release. Whisperer copies the text and shows a Copied ⌘V toast. Press ⌘V in the prompt box and send.
</Step>
</StepList>

There's nothing to configure per tool. The same paste works in an editor panel, a terminal or a browser.

## Prompts, not syntax

Whisperer writes what you say as text. It doesn't convert "open paren" into `(` or "camel case" into camelCase, and in vibe coding you don't need it to: the agent writes the syntax. If you want to mention an existing function, add it to the dictionary once ("use effect" to useEffect) and it'll come out right in every prompt.

| You're doing | Use |
|---|---|
| Describing a feature or fix | Voice |
| Redirecting the agent mid-task | Voice |
| Review comments | Voice |
| Small edits to the generated code | Keyboard |
| Navigating the diff | Keyboard |

## Let the agent read your context

Specs often live in meetings and voice notes, not tickets. With Pro, Whisperer's local [MCP server](/mcp) lets Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Windsurf search your meeting transcripts and dictation history. "Read yesterday's call with design and build what we agreed for the empty state" becomes a prompt the agent can act on. Whisperer's [meeting notes](/meetings) record calls without a bot joining.
