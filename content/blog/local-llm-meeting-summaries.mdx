You can get meeting summaries with no internet connection by running a small language model on your Mac. In Whisperer, the built-in model is **Qwen3.5 4B**, about a 3.2 GB download, running on Apple Silicon. It writes the overview, decisions, action items and open questions after every call, and nothing leaves the Mac. It's the default choice, it's free, and it's the fallback whenever a cloud AI isn't available.

## Why summarize meetings locally

A meeting transcript is often the most sensitive text you produce all week. It has names, numbers, plans that aren't public yet and things people said because they thought only the room was listening.

With a local model, the whole chain stays on your Mac:

- the call audio and your microphone are recorded locally
- transcription and speaker labels run locally
- the summary is written locally

There's no account, no upload and no server log to worry about. For a board update, a performance review or a call with a lawyer, that's often the only setup people are comfortable with.

## What a 4B model does well

A 4-billion-parameter model is small next to the models behind ChatGPT or Claude. It's still good at the parts of meeting notes that are mostly about structure:

- pulling out who agreed to do what
- listing decisions that were said out loud
- writing a short overview of a 30-minute call
- tidying a transcript so it reads cleanly

For a weekly sync, a standup, a customer call with a clear agenda or a one-on-one, the local notes are often all you need.

## Where cloud models still win

It would be dishonest to say a laptop model matches a frontier cloud model. It doesn't, and here's where you'll notice:

### Long meetings

A two-hour planning session produces a long transcript. Larger cloud models hold more of it in view at once and tend to connect a point from minute 10 with a decision at minute 95.

### Nuance and tone

When a decision was half-made, or someone disagreed politely, bigger models are better at writing "Dan had concerns about the date; not settled" instead of turning it into a clean decision.

### Speed

How fast the local model writes depends on your Mac: the chip and how much memory it has. On a Mac with plenty of memory it's comfortable. On a base model with other heavy apps open, it's slower. A cloud model's speed depends on the provider instead.

## Picking between local and cloud

You don't have to choose once. Whisperer lets you set a default and keep any single meeting local.

| Situation | Good fit |
|---|---|
| Confidential call (HR, legal, finance) | On this Mac |
| No internet (plane, train, poor hotel Wi-Fi) | On this Mac |
| Short sync or standup | On this Mac |
| Long workshop or planning session | A cloud AI you already pay for |
| You want the most detailed notes possible | A cloud AI you already pay for |

If you do use a cloud AI, it's your own plan: Claude, ChatGPT, Codex or Gemini, or an API key. Only the text transcript goes to it. Audio never leaves the Mac. See [bring your own AI for meeting notes](/blog/bring-your-own-ai-meeting-notes) for that setup.

## FAQ

### Does the local model need an internet connection?

Only once, to download it. After that, summaries, Ask AI and transcript polish run with no connection.

### How big is the local model?

Qwen3.5 4B is about a 3.2 GB download and runs on Apple Silicon Macs.

### Is a local model as good as ChatGPT or Claude?

Not on every meeting. For clear, structured calls it does well. For long, messy meetings with a lot of nuance, large cloud models usually write better notes. You can switch per meeting.
