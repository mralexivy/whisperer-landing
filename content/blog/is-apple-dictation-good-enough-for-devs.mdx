Apple Dictation is good enough if you dictate a sentence or two at a time, in one language, using everyday words. It is free, it is already on your Mac, and it types straight into the field you're in. It stops being enough when you dictate for minutes at a time, use names and jargon it keeps getting wrong, switch languages, or want a record of what you said. That's the gap a dedicated app such as [Whisperer](/features) fills, for $2.99.

## What Apple Dictation does well

Give Apple credit first. On a modern Mac, built-in dictation is better than its reputation.

- **Zero setup.** Press the dictation key (or the microphone key on newer keyboards) and talk. Nothing to install or buy.
- **It types where you are.** Text appears at the cursor in Mail, Notes, Messages or a browser field.
- **On-device for many languages.** On Apple Silicon, a lot of dictation runs locally, so it's quick and private.
- **Same on every device.** The habit you build on iPhone works on your Mac.
- **Editing by voice.** You can say punctuation and use spoken commands to select and fix text.

For replying to a text or adding a calendar note, that is plenty. If that describes your week, keep using it.

## Where it falls short

### Long passages

Apple Dictation is tuned for short bursts. When you talk for a minute or more, as you would drafting an email or a document, accuracy tends to drift, and pauses can end the session before you're done.

### Names, products and jargon

Your colleague's surname, your company's product, a medical or legal term: these are where built-in dictation trips most often. You can add some words to your contacts and text replacements, but there's no real dictionary that learns from the corrections you make.

### More than one language

You choose a dictation language in Settings. If you think in English and reply to a client in Spanish, you switch languages by hand or get gibberish.

### No memory

Once the text is in the field, it's gone. There's no history to search, no audio to replay, and no way to transcribe an audio file you already have.

### Meetings

Apple Dictation is for your own voice in a text field. It won't record a Zoom or Teams call, tell you who said what, or write up decisions and action items afterwards.

## FAQ

### Is Apple Dictation good enough for everyday use?

For short messages, quick notes and search queries, yes. It is free, built into macOS, and types straight into the field. It is weaker on long passages, names and jargon, and switching languages mid-thought.

### Does Apple Dictation work offline on a Mac?

On Apple Silicon Macs, dictation runs on the device for many languages. Some languages and features still need a connection, so check Apple's support pages for yours.
