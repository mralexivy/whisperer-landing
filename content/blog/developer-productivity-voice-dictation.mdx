A lot of a developer's day is writing words that aren't code: Slack replies, PR descriptions, docs, tickets, and more and more, prompts to AI coding agents. All of it is plain language. All of it can be spoken.

How much time that saves depends on how much text you write. Here's a way to estimate it for yourself, and an honest list of where voice doesn't help.

## The basic numbers

Two widely quoted figures:

- Conversational speech runs at roughly **130 to 150 words per minute**.
- Average typing speed is usually put at around **40 words per minute**; experienced typists often reach 60 to 80.

A 2016 Stanford study on smartphones found speech input about three times faster than typing on a phone keyboard. On a full keyboard the gap is smaller, but it's still large for prose.

<Callout type="info">
Raw speaking speed isn't the whole story. You also spend a few seconds checking and pasting each dictation, and fixing the odd misheard word. In practice a 2x gain on prose is a reasonable expectation; more if you type slowly, less if you're very fast.
</Callout>

## A worked estimate

This table is an example, not a measurement. Change the word counts to match your own day. It assumes 40 wpm typing and an effective 100 wpm by voice, after allowing for checking and fixes.

| Activity | Words a day | Typing | Voice | Saved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AI prompts | 1,500 | 38 min | 15 min | 23 min |
| Slack and Teams | 1,000 | 25 min | 10 min | 15 min |
| PRs, reviews, tickets | 800 | 20 min | 8 min | 12 min |
| Docs and comments | 600 | 15 min | 6 min | 9 min |
| Email | 400 | 10 min | 4 min | 6 min |
| **Total** | **4,300** | **108 min** | **43 min** | **65 min** |

If you write half as much, halve the result. If you type at 70 wpm, the saving shrinks to about 20 minutes. Whisperer's **Statistics** page shows how many words you actually dictate, so after a week you can replace the guesses with your own numbers.

## Staying in flow

Speed is one part. The other is fewer interruptions.

### Reply without leaving the task

Someone pings you mid-task. Click into Slack, hold Fn, say the reply, release, press ⌘V. Fifteen seconds, and your head is still in the code.

### Longer prompts without the effort

When typing, people trim prompts to the minimum. When speaking, they explain. Agents do better with the explanation, so you spend less time on follow-up corrections.

## Where voice doesn't help

- **Writing syntax.** Whisperer writes what you say as text. It has no spoken symbol or casing commands, and dictating code character by character is slower than typing. Let the agent write the code, or type it. See [how to dictate code on Mac](/blog/how-to-dictate-code-on-mac).
- **Small edits and navigation.** Keyboard and shortcuts win.
- **Open-plan offices.** Talking to your computer all day isn't always welcome. A headset and a quieter corner help.

## Your hands

Typing thousands of words a day adds up over years. Moving prose to voice takes a large share of that load off your wrists. If your hands already hurt, see a doctor; voice can help but isn't treatment. More in the [RSI prevention guide](/blog/rsi-prevention-voice-coding).

## A voice-heavy day

- **Morning:** dictate the first agent prompt describing what you're building today.
- **Standup:** speak your update into Slack.
- **Code review:** dictate review comments, paste them on the lines.
- **Docs:** speak a README section; with Pro, run it through the Rewrite or Format AI mode.
- **Afternoon:** keep prompting Claude Code or Cursor by voice.
