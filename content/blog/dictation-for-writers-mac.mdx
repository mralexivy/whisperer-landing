Dictation helps writers because most people speak two to three times faster than they type, and speaking keeps you from editing every sentence as you write it. On a Mac, Whisperer turns your speech into text on the device, cleans it up with an AI mode if you want, and copies it so you paste with ⌘V into Docs, Word, Scrivener, Obsidian or anywhere else. Your drafts never leave the Mac unless you choose a cloud AI.

The catch is that dictated text needs editing. So does typed text. The question is whether you'd rather spend your time producing raw material or refining it.

## Why dictation works for writing

### Speed

Typing speeds for most people sit somewhere around 40 to 60 words per minute. Conversational speech is often well over 120. Here's what that gap looks like for a first draft, assuming 50 WPM typing and 140 WPM speaking:

| Output | Typing | Speaking |
|---|---|---|
| Blog post (1,500 words) | 30 min | 11 min |
| Newsletter (800 words) | 16 min | 6 min |
| Book chapter (3,000 words) | 60 min | 21 min |

<Callout type="tip">
These are raw first-draft times. You'll still edit, but you'll have more material to edit, sooner.
</Callout>

### Flow

Typing creates a feedback loop: you see words appear, you judge them, you backspace, you slow down. Speaking breaks that loop. A lot of writers find their dictated drafts sound more like how they actually talk.

### Your hands get a break

Long typing days are hard on wrists and shoulders. Dictation lets you produce the same draft with less keyboard time.

## Setting up Whisperer for writing

<StepList>
<Step num="1" title="Pick an AI mode">
In Workspace, open **AI Modes** and choose what happens to your dictation after it's transcribed. For writing, the useful ones are **Rewrite** (spoken language into cleaner prose), **Format** (Markdown headings and bullets), and **Grammar** (punctuation and spelling, your words unchanged). Or leave it on plain dictation with Fast Polish.
</Step>

<Step num="2" title="Pick the AI that runs it">
On the **LLM** page, keep the built-in Qwen3.5 4B model, which runs offline on your Mac, or use your own Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini plan. See [your AI](/ai).
</Step>

<Step num="3" title="Build your dictionary">
Add character names, place names and jargon to your [personal dictionary](/blog/personal-dictionary-setup). It makes a big difference for fiction and specialist writing. Whisperer also learns words from your corrections.
</Step>

<Step num="4" title="Choose a mode for long sessions">
Hold-to-talk is fine for a paragraph. For longer stretches, switch to toggle (press once to start, once to stop) or hands-free in Settings.
</Step>
</StepList>

## Workflows

### Brain dump

1. Start recording and talk about your topic. Don't think about structure.
2. Stop. Whisperer transcribes the whole thing.
3. With Rewrite selected, you get cleaner prose to paste. Without it, you get the raw words.
4. Edit from there.

This separates creating from editing, which is usually where writing gets slow.

### Section by section

1. Write your outline with section headings.
2. Dictate one section at a time.
3. Edit the complete draft.

Works well for blog posts, articles, anything with a clear structure.

### Walking draft

1. Put on a headset with a decent mic.
2. Switch to toggle mode.
3. Walk around the room and talk.
4. Sit down with a first draft.

Researchers at Stanford (Oppezzo and Schwartz, 2014) found that walking increased people's scores on creative-thinking tasks. Walking and talking is an easy way to get that benefit while drafting.

### Interviews and recorded notes

Have a recorded interview or a voice memo from your phone? Drop it into **File Transcription** and get text back, offline. See [transcribing audio files on a Mac](/blog/transcribe-audio-files-offline-mac).

## AI modes for writers

| Mode | What it does |
|---|---|
| Rewrite | Turns spoken language into readable prose and tightens sentences, keeping your meaning |
| Format | Adds Markdown headings, bullets and numbered lists for Notion, Obsidian and similar tools |
| Grammar | Fixes punctuation and spelling without rewriting |
| Summarize | Condenses a long voice note into key points |
| Translate | Dictate in one language, get text in another |

With the built-in model, all of this runs on your Mac.

## Why offline matters

<Callout type="info">
Cloud dictation services send your voice to remote servers. For unpublished manuscripts, client work or anything personal, that's a problem. Whisperer turns speech into text on your Mac. If you pick a cloud AI for rewriting, only the text goes to it, never the audio.
</Callout>

This matters if you're:

- An author with unpublished drafts
- A journalist with confidential sources
- Working under an NDA
- Writing anything you don't want stored on someone else's server

## Tips

1. **Pause between sentences, not mid-sentence.** It makes transcription cleaner.
2. **Dictate sections, not the whole piece.** Easier to manage.
3. **Let Fast Polish handle punctuation and fillers.** You don't need to say "comma."
4. **Build your dictionary** with character names, places and jargon.
5. **Check the history.** Every dictation is saved in Workspace with its audio, so nothing is lost if you forget to paste.
